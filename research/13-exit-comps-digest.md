## 3. Venture return math (ARITHMETIC VERIFIED CORRECT)

The report's math checks out end to end. Reproduced so it can be reused.

**Setup:** pre-seed ownership 8–15%; dilution path 12–15% → 8–12% → 6–9% → **4–7% at exit**. Worked example uses 15% initial, ~60% total dilution → **6% at exit** (and a 4% sensitivity case).

| Exit value | Founder/fund proceeds @ 6% | @ 4% |
|---|---|---|
| $10M | **$0.6M** | $0.4M / ($0.25M note below) |
| $25M | $1.5M | — |
| $40M | $2.4M | — |
| $50M | $3.0M | — |
| $100M | $6.0M | — |

**Fund-returner framing (this is the number that matters to a pre-seed investor):** required exit value = fund size ÷ ownership at exit.

| To return a... | at 6% exit ownership | at 4% |
|---|---|---|
| $10M fund | **$167M exit** | $250M |
| $25M fund | $417M | $625M |
| $40M fund | $667M | $1.0B |
| $50M fund | $833M | $1.25B |
| $100M fund | **$1.67B** | $2.5B |

**Sanity check the report runs and gets right:** a **$610M exit × 6% = $36.6M** — which returns a **$25M fund, not a $50M fund.** In other words, even the category-defining comp in this space (The Browser Company's $610M) only returns a small fund at realistic dilution. Cited framework: Harlem Capital's fund-returner model, $40M-fund example.

**Verification:** 15% × (1 − 60%) = 6% ✓; $10M/6% = $167M ✓; $100M/6% = $1.67B ✓; $610M × 6% = $36.6M ✓. All correct.

| Exceptional | $150M–$600M | Loom ($975M is above this), HelloSign-plus |
| Category-defining | **$600M+** | **The Browser Company ($610M)** |

**The report's blunt conclusion:**
> outcomes are "more concentrated in the $0–$50M range than the billion-dollar range."

This is the honest, investor-credible framing: the realistic modal outcome is a sub-$50M strategic acquisition; $600M+ is the rare ceiling, not the plan. Pairing that with the fund-returner math (§3) gives the true pitch tension: a $600M exit is category-defining *and still only returns a $25M fund* at realistic dilution — so the return story only works for a small fund or with an above-median outcome.

## 5. Report Quality Problems

Genuinely the strongest report of the three. Problems are real but mostly disclosed by the report itself.

### 1. Private-company revenue leans on data aggregators (but the report flags it)
Private ARR/revenue estimates draw on `getlatka`, `owler`, `salary.com`, `rocketreach`, `prospeo`, `datanyze`, `privco`, `tracxn`. These are unreliable for private financials. **To the report's credit, it labels these as estimates and hedges ("reported, not confirmed," "estimates, not facts").** Do not present any aggregator-derived ARR as a hard number, but the report did not pretend otherwise.

### 2. Grammarly's $700M ARR is paired with an SEO farm
The ~$700M ARR figure is cited alongside **`sqmagazine.co.uk` [86]**, an SEO content site. The number may be roughly right (Grammarly is large) but the citation is not investor-grade. Verify against a primary/press source before use.

### 3. A few content/aggregator domains carry secondary color
`operatorbook.dev`, `fueler.io`, `versaedits.com`, `readthesignal.co` appear among sources. None carries a headline number, but they lower the average source quality.

### 4. "Undisclosed" prices invite over-precise retelling
Notion/Cron ("eight-figure," unconfirmed), OpenAI/Rockset ("nine-figure"), Meta/Limitless (undisclosed) — the report is careful to mark these unconfirmed. Preserve that hedge; do not convert "nine-figure reported" into a specific number on a slide.

### 5. JetBrains revenue is an entity figure, not consolidated
The ~$649M (15.07B CZK) is the Czech entity's revenue, **not** the consolidated group. The report notes this; don't cite it as "JetBrains revenue" full stop.

### 6. Citation padding
~131 of 222 sources (~59%) are never cited in the body. By raw count the biggest padding of the three, though the ~91 cited sources are of high quality (Reuters, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, SEC, BusinessWire, official company blogs).

**What's genuinely strong:** the terminated-Adobe/Figma details (Reuters ARR, $1B breakup fee), The Browser Company $610M (matches Atlassian's public number), Loom $975M, HelloSign $230M, and the entire venture-math section are well-sourced and correct. This report's numbers are the safest of the three to build a slide on — with the aggregator caveats respected.
