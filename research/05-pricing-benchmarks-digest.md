## 2. THE CRUX — can a purely LOCAL Mac utility (no cloud/collab) sustain a subscription?

**The report's own evidence says no.** Every local Mac tool that successfully charges a subscription attaches it to a **cloud, AI, sync, or bundle hook**; every purely-local, no-cloud utility monetizes as a **one-time purchase**.

| Charges a subscription (local tool) | Justified by | One-time only (pure local) |
|---|---|---|
| Raycast $8/mo | AI / cloud sync / themes | Things 3 $49.99 (anti-sub) |
| Setapp $9.99–22.99/mo | 200+ app bundle | Alfred £34 (~$45) |
| Craft $4.99–15/mo | sync / collab / AI | CleanShot X base $29 |
| Bear $2.99/mo | iCloud sync / themes | TablePlus $99 (Pro sub optional) |
| Timing $10/mo | AI categorization / reports | |
| Rize $9.99–39.99/mo | AI insights / cloud sync | |
| CleanShot X Cloud Pro $8–10/mo | **cloud hosting only** (base is one-time) | |
| Warp $18–50/mo | AI credits / cloud | |

The report's recommendation point #7 concedes this directly: to sustain a subscription you must **"bundle at least two of AI / cloud sync / collaboration / analytics."** A $10–25/mo Mac subscription with **no cloud component** has **zero supporting precedent** in this dataset. This is the single most important finding for Evo.

## 6. Mac App Store vs. direct distribution

| Factor | Mac App Store | Direct |
|---|---|---|
| Platform fee | **15%** first year, then 15% for small devs (<$1M/yr); **30%** standard | **0%** (payment processor only) |
| Technical | Sandboxing required; limited entitlements (no background agents, restricted system APIs); review | No sandbox; full system access; no review |
| Payments | Apple IAP, constrained | Full (Stripe/Paddle/etc.) |
| Tax/VAT | Apple handles EU VAT | You handle, or use MoR |

Most pro Mac utilities go **direct** (Raycast, Alfred, Things, CleanShot, Timing, Rize, Bear, Craft) because sandboxing breaks launchers/automation/screen-recording/time-tracking. [90][91]

**CAC:** every row is flagged **UNRELIABLE / no prosumer-specific data** — content/SEO $0–50, community $0–20 (organic), PH launch $0–500 (100–1,000 signups in 48h), paid social $50–300+, paid search $30–200+. No trustworthy prosumer-desktop CAC exists in the report.

## 8. The pricing recommendation — WITH ARITHMETIC CHECK

**Recommendation:** "$10–15/mo (annual billing), or $12–18/mo (monthly)." Free tier core forever; **Pro $12/mo or $120/yr (20% discount)**; Team $15–20/user/mo. Use Lemon Squeezy or Paddle. Justify with ≥2 of AI/sync/team/analytics.

**Arithmetic errors (both in the annual-discount claims):**

1. **"20–25% discount for annual (e.g., $120/yr vs $15/mo)."** $15 × 12 = **$180**. ($180 − $120) / $180 = **33.3%** — not 20–25%. The example **contradicts the stated rule**.
2. **"Pro tier: $12/mo or $120/yr (20% discount)."** $12 × 12 = **$144**. ($144 − $120) / $144 = **16.7%** — labeled 20%. A true 20% off $144 would be **$115.20/yr**.
3. **Internal conflation:** $120/yr = **$10/mo** effective, but it is paired with a **$12/mo** monthly anchor — the annual plan's effective monthly sits below the monthly price it is "discounting."

## 9. Report Quality Problems

1. **47 uncited sources** ([104]–[150]) — including the exact official pricing pages the brief required (Things [137], CleanShot [138], Superhuman [139], Linear [135], Granola [136]) sit unused while the body cites SEO aggregators.
2. **Pricing sourced to SEO content farms / synthetic domains** — costuse, spendchime, get-alfred.ai, icebox.cool, outflo.io, thisandthat.chat, layer3labs.io, cmdk.email, aimojo.io, saaspricehub.io, ki-auf-dem-mac.pages.dev, typefire, nextriva, operatorbook, efficient.app, unknowncharges, getapps.cafe (×6), macappsdaily — violating the report's own "use the company pricing page" rule.
3. **"Recurly" churn misattribution** — the "Recurly benchmark" is linked to `churn.io` [75] and `recurx.app` [76], not recurly.com.
4. **Notion→Linear URL mismatch** — Notion pricing cited to `opendecision.com/products/linear` [37].
5. **Two arithmetic errors** in the pricing recommendation's discount math (33% and 16.7% mislabeled as "20–25%" and "20%").
6. **Load-bearing waitlist stats** (3% / 10%) rest on a **single analyst** (Gaurav Vohra) with no primary link.
7. **CAC and most churn data are absent or self-flagged unreliable** — honest, but leaves the monetization case thin.
8. **The headline premise is unsupported:** no purely-local, no-cloud Mac subscription exists in the dataset; every one-time-purchase tool is the pure-local case.
