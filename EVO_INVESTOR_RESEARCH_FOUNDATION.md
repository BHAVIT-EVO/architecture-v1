## 2. EVO CATEGORY ANALYSIS

### What Evo Actually Is (Product Truth)

Evo is a **local-first cognitive continuity layer** that:
1. Observes computer activity
2. Understands what constitutes meaningful work
3. Preserves that understanding as persistent Places
4. Restores the native context needed to continue that work

### The Core Insight

**The computer forgets.**

Every time you context-switch, close applications, or end a session, the continuity of your work is lost. You manually reconstruct what you were doing—reopening tabs, finding files, remembering where you left off.

The computer treats work as isolated artifacts (files, tabs, windows) rather than coherent bodies of work with continuity across time.

### The Novel Abstraction: The Place

Not a workspace, not a project folder, not a tab group—a **Place** is:
- A living representation of a body of work
- Emerges from witnessed activity rather than manual construction
- Maintains identity and continuation state across sessions
- Understands what matters now vs what's historical
- Can restore relevant native context intelligently
- Persists across interruptions and devices

### Category Positioning Problem

Evo sits at the intersection of multiple categories but fits cleanly into **none**:

**NOT quite:**
- **Productivity software** – Too narrow; Evo isn't about tasks or notes
- **AI assistant** – Doesn't chat or respond to commands as primary interface
- **Computer-use agent** – Doesn't autonomously execute tasks
- **Session manager** – Too shallow; browser tabs only
- **Knowledge management** – Doesn't organize information hierarchies
- **Screen recorder** – Creates privacy concerns and misses the cognitive model

**ACTUALLY:**
- **Personal computing infrastructure**
- **Cognitive continuity layer**

**Investor Mental Bucket:**  
Most likely initially categorized as "personal AI" or "AI productivity," but the pitch must expand that bucket to include the larger computing infrastructure opportunity.

If positioned as just "AI productivity," Evo looks like a Notion feature. The pitch must establish that continuity across native applications is a **new foundational layer**.

### Current MVP State (Legitimate Claims)

**Working on macOS:**
- Observes activity (apps, windows, URLs, files, focus)
- Constructs Moments from continuous activity streams
- Derives Places from observed work patterns
- Presents continuation surface with Resume Point and Next Step
- Executes native restoration with preflight, binding, ordering, reporting
- Local-first architecture with SQLite + AES-256-GCM
- Onboarding that remembers user, speaks sequentially, personalizes
- Browser extension for richer tab context
- REST API for local daemon coordination

**Legitimate Technical Achievements:**
- Separation of canonical understanding from transient execution
- Plan/act restoration model
- Deterministic ordering and partial execution
- Honest reporting (doesn't claim success when it failed)
- Execution results don't contaminate memory
- Platform-neutral cognitive model with platform-specific executors

### What CANNOT Be Claimed Yet

**Do NOT claim:**
- Production-ready cross-device sync (architecture exists, not shipped)
- Automation or workflow repetition (future vision, not current)
- Voice interface beyond introduction (wake phrase is planned)
- Windows/Linux parity (stubbed/partial)
- Proven user retention or engagement metrics
- Revenue or monetization validation
- Network effects or viral growth

## 4. FUNDED PITCH DECK RESEARCH

### Pre-Seed / Seed Deck Patterns from 2024-2026

Analysis of 100+ funded AI startup pitch decks from pre-seed and seed rounds reveals consistent patterns:

### What Works

**Narrative:**
- **Founder insight on slide 1-2:** "Here's what I noticed" not "here's the problem everyone knows"
- **Personal connection:** Founder has lived the problem
- **Product before market:** Show the solution, then justify the size
- **Honest current state:** "Here's what works; here's what we're building next"
- **Expansion tease:** "Starts as X, becomes Y" in one sentence, not five slides

**Visual:**
- **Opening slide:** One sentence, often provocative or clear positioning
- **Product slide:** Large, annotated screenshot showing the "aha"
- **Traction slide:** If exists, show one compelling metric (not six mediocre ones)
- **Team slide:** Photos + relevant background, not resume
- **Closing slide:** The ask, clearly stated

**Structure:**
- **10-12 slides total:** Attention span is limited; every slide must change investor belief
- **One metric per slide:** Dense slides get skipped; singular focus drives comprehension
- **Bottom-up market sizing:** "$X per seat × Y target users" beats "$50B TAM by 2030"

### What Fails

- Generic problem statements everyone's heard
- TAM slides with no connection to actual go-to-market
- Feature lists without user benefit
- Architecture diagrams before product benefit
- Competitor logo walls (shows paranoia, not differentiation)
- Vague traction ("early users love it" vs "12 daily actives, 40% return rate")
- AI buzzwords without technical specificity
- Templates that look AI-generated

### Pre-Seed Specific Insights

Pre-seed is fundamentally different from seed. Investors are betting on:

1. **Founder-market fit:** Why is this founder uniquely positioned to see and solve this?
2. **Technical insight:** What do you know that others don't?
3. **Working prototype:** Can you actually build this?
4. **Clarity of thinking:** Do you understand the wedge vs platform distinction?

**You do NOT need:**
- Revenue
- Paying customers
- Market validation at scale
- Competitive analysis depth

**You DO need:**
- Honest demo of what works today
- Clear articulation of the insight
- Evidence you can execute (speed of iteration, technical competence)
- Specific next milestones

## 5. PIXEL-LEVEL DESIGN RESEARCH

### Visual Language of Successful AI Decks

Analysis of design patterns in funded AI startup decks reveals a shift from 2021-era dense SaaS decks to cleaner, more visual storytelling:

### Typography

- **Headline font:** Sans-serif, bold, 32-48pt for key statements
- **Body text:** 14-18pt, generous line height (1.5-1.8)
- **Hierarchy:** 2-3 font sizes maximum per slide
- **Line length:** 50-75 characters maximum for readability
- **Common choices:** Inter, Helvetica Neue, SF Pro, or custom brand fonts

### Layout

- **Margins:** Generous whitespace (10-15% of slide on all sides)
- **Density:** 1 key point per slide, not 5
- **Grid:** Consistent alignment, usually 2-3 column layouts
- **Visual hierarchy:** Clear primary element (headline or product shot)

### Color

- **2024-2026 trend:** Away from blue gradients, toward distinctive brand colors
- **Dark mode:** Increasingly common for technical products
- **Contrast:** Text must be readable—avoid low-contrast "aesthetic" choices
- **Accent color:** Used sparingly for emphasis

### Product Screenshots

- **Size:** Large enough to read—50-70% of slide area
- **Context:** Usually shown in device frame (browser window, phone, desktop app)
- **Annotations:** Arrows and callouts used sparingly, only when necessary
- **Quality:** High-resolution, actual product (not mockups unless pre-launch)
- **Cropping:** Focus on the key feature, not the entire UI

### Diagrams

- **Simplicity:** 3-5 nodes maximum
- **Purpose:** Show flow or relationship, not completeness
- **Style:** Clean geometric shapes, minimal decoration

### Overall Density Pattern

Winning decks follow a "breathing" rhythm:

- **Slide 1:** Sparse (just positioning statement)
- **Slide 2-3:** Moderate (problem + insight)
- **Slide 4-5:** Dense (product demo)
- **Slide 6-8:** Moderate (traction, market, team)
- **Slide 9-10:** Sparse (vision, ask)

## 7. INVESTOR PSYCHOLOGY

### The Pre-Seed Decision Chain

At pre-seed, investors aren't evaluating business fundamentals—they're pattern-matching against successful early bets they've made or missed.

The belief chain Evo must construct:

1. **"This founder sees something I don't"** → Insight on slide 1-2
2. **"The problem is real and painful"** → User pain, not market statistics
3. **"This solution is non-obvious"** → Why hasn't this been built before?
4. **"They can actually build it"** → Working demo
5. **"The wedge can become a platform"** → Expansion story (but not the opening)
6. **"The timing is right"** → Why now? (technology unlock, behavior change, or market shift)
7. **"This team can execute"** → Speed, clarity, product taste
8. **"I don't want to miss this"** → Scarcity, momentum, or FOMO

### Psychological Triggers That Work

- **Recognition:** "I've felt this pain"
- **Envy:** "I wish I'd thought of that"
- **Inevitability:** "Of course this should exist"
- **Founder conviction:** "They're building this regardless of funding"

### Anti-Triggers to Avoid

- Privacy fear ("this feels like surveillance")
- Category confusion ("what is this actually?")
- Vaporware smell ("they're describing not building")
- Founder hesitation ("they haven't thought through the hard parts")
- Competitive despair ("Microsoft will just copy this")

## 12. RECOMMENDED POSITIONING

> **Revised 2026-09-01** against the developer-tooling framing decision and the two evidence overrides in `EVO_EVIDENCE_RECONCILIATION.md` §8. The core "continuity layer" idea survives — but it moves out of the *opening line* and into the *investor argument*, the beachhead gets named, and the paid tier gets a hook.

### The two registers — say the narrow thing first

The category-language research (§8.1b) found that every winner it studied started with a narrow, concrete self-description and earned the grand category language later. The one company that led with "a brand new category of software" — Arc — is the one that died. So Evo needs two positioning registers, used in this order.

**1. The opening self-description** (narrow, concrete — the first sentence anyone hears):

**"Evo gets you back into any project — every file, tab, and window — exactly where you left off."**

This is the *job*, stated plainly. It puts Evo on the "entering a category" side of the test below: the buyer already knows the pain and can already name adjacent tools (tab managers, Rewind, session savers), so Evo is *entering* a category, not creating one — the cheaper, faster, historically-survivable path.

**2. The investor / architecture framing** (the argument — only after the job lands):

"Evo is the continuity layer for how you work. Current systems remember files and processes; Evo remembers *work*. It starts with restoration, and the same understanding expands into anticipation and workflow automation. Local-first by design."

The infrastructure / "cognitive layer" language belongs *here*, in the why-this-is-venture-scale argument — not in the one-liner. (This reverses the previous draft, which led with "'cognitive layer' establishes infrastructure positioning." Leading with infrastructure language is precisely the Arc move the research warns against.)

### The buyer test (usable verbatim, from the research)

> "If the buyer already understands the problem and can name several existing solutions, you are probably entering a category. If the first sales conversation must establish why the problem matters at all, you are attempting category creation."

Evo is on the "entering" side. Reference the pain as already understood; don't build the deck as if it must first prove the problem exists. Slack's model applies: sell "the ability to ride a horse better," not the saddle.

### Name the beachhead — "your computer" is too broad

The framing decision (developer / technical-knowledge-worker tooling) means positioning can't stop at "across your computer." Only ~33% of developers are on macOS, so the wedge needs a precise first user: which kind of technical worker, doing what, juggling how many concurrent projects, on a Mac. This is still unresolved and is the first thing to nail in the slide outline — but the positioning language should speak to that specific person, not "knowledge workers" in general.

### The paid tier needs a hook — do not position as a local-only subscription

Pricing/GTM research (§8.1a) found no precedent for a pure-local, no-cloud Mac subscription. Local-first is the *trust* story, not the *billing* story. Positioning must not imply the recurring price buys "local software" — it buys the encrypted-sync / AI / team layer on top. Keep "local-first by design" as the trust claim; pair the paid tier with its actual hook.

### Why the core line still works (as the argument, not the opener)

1. **"Continuity layer"** frames infrastructure, not an app — but deployed as the *argument*, after the concrete job, not as the first words.
2. **"Remembers work, not files"** is the one-sentence differentiator against tab managers, recent-files, and note tools.
3. **"Local-first by design"** carries trust (post-Recall) — stated as trust, never as a data moat (see §13.3).

## 13. RECOMMENDED INVESTOR THESIS

The thesis an investor must believe to invest in Evo at pre-seed:

### 1. The Computer's Memory Gap Is a Real Problem

Returning to a project after switching away means reconstructing what you were doing — which files, which tabs, which train of thought. Current solutions (browser tabs, recent files, window managers) are primitive: they remember artifacts, not work.

> **Do not quantify this with a minutes-saved figure** (revised 2026-09-01). The "23 minutes" / "15–30 minutes" stat is unsupported — it is not in any Gloria Mark paper (`EVO_EVIDENCE_RECONCILIATION.md` §1.1) — and the interruption literature can be used to *refute* a time-savings claim (§1.3). Sell the cognitive and emotional burden of reconstruction, demonstrated by real users, not a stat.

### 2. Passive Observation Beats Manual Capture

Knowledge management products (Notion, Roam, Obsidian) require manual input. Evo's insight is that the computer already sees everything—the missing piece is understanding, not capture.

### 3. Local-First Is Trust, Not a Data Moat

Post-Recall backlash, privacy-first architecture is a competitive advantage — for *trust and adoption*. But local-first is explicitly **not** a data moat (revised 2026-09-01; `EVO_EVIDENCE_RECONCILIATION.md` §8.4, §1.4): there is no proprietary cloud corpus and no cross-user learning loop. The real defensibility is trust, accumulated workflow configuration, retrieval quality, and the switching cost of the context a user builds up over time. State the moat as trust + lock-in + restoration quality — never as "proprietary data."

### 4. The Wedge Is Real: Work Restoration Is Immediately Valuable

Unlike pure memory products (Rewind) that required search, Evo's restoration capability provides immediate utility. "Continue this work" is more valuable than "search your past."

### 5. The Platform Expansion Path Exists

Once Evo understands your work patterns:
- (a) intelligent anticipation
- (b) recurring workflow detection
- (c) automation

The cognitive graph built from restoration becomes the foundation for assistance.

### 6. Cross-Device Continuity Unlocks Consumer Persistence

If Evo remains Mac-only, it's niche. Encrypted cross-device sync (via iCloud) means work continuity follows the user across their computing environment—that's when it becomes essential infrastructure.

### 7. This Team Can Execute the Technical Challenges

The product requires OS-level integration, sophisticated cognitive modeling, and reliable native restoration. The working macOS prototype proves technical feasibility.

### 8. Timing: The Computer-Memory Category Just Cleared

Rewind raised substantially, pivoted to a wearable (Limitless), was acquired by Meta, and shut down its Mac product in Dec 2025; Recall is privacy-toxic — leaving a vacuum.

> **Never assert "Rewind failed" on retention or demand** (revised 2026-09-01; §1.5). No public cohort data exists; the only defensible claim is that the Mac product no longer exists. Note too that the white space narrowed in Aug 2026 — nine capture products shipped, including OpenAI's (§1.4) — so the moat is classification + restoration, not capture, and the "no one is doing this" line is dead.

Behavior change (AI normalization) + technical capability (local LLMs) + competitive void = **now is the moment**.

### Investment Thesis Summary

"Evo starts as the tool that gets you back into any project exactly where you left off, and grows into the continuity layer personal computing lacks. The current MVP proves technical feasibility and immediate user value (work restoration). Local-first architecture earns the trust that competitors' privacy stances cost them. Platform expansion from restoration → anticipation → automation is the venture-scale argument. Team has demonstrated execution capability. This is the right team, right timing, right wedge."

> **Two honesty checks on this summary** (added 2026-09-01): (a) "large outcome potential" is contested — the realistic modal exit is $0–50M and even the category-defining $610M comp returns only a ~$25M fund at realistic dilution (§8.7), so the return story is calibrated to small pre-seed funds, not a blanket "large outcome." (b) The narrow opener ("the tool that gets you back into your work") leads; the "continuity layer" is the argument that follows — never the first sentence (§8.1b).

## 14. WEDGE ANALYSIS

### The Narrowest Viable Wedge

**"Return to your work exactly where you left off—without reconstructing context"**

### Why This Wedge Works

1. **Immediately understandable:** No explanation needed—everyone has felt this pain
2. **Demonstrable in 30 seconds:** Close everything → Click Continue → Work restored
3. **Tangible value:** The reconstruction step is eliminated, not shortened — you land back in the work instead of rebuilding it. (State this as the removed burden, demonstrated live; do not attach a minutes-saved figure — see §13.1.)
4. **Doesn't require behavior change:** User works normally, Evo observes
5. **Platform foundation:** Restoration requires cognitive understanding, which enables everything else

### What the Wedge is NOT

❌ "Search your computer history" (Rewind's wedge—requires remembering what to search for)  
❌ "Manage all your tasks" (becomes task manager, crowded category)  
❌ "Automate your workflows" (too far ahead, implies more than MVP delivers)  
❌ "Never lose context" (passive benefit, not active value)

### Target Users for Wedge

1. **Developers:** Context-switch between features, bugs, code reviews constantly
2. **Designers:** Multiple projects, each with design files, references, feedback
3. **Researchers:** Deep work sessions across papers, notes, experiments
4. **Consultants:** Client projects with distinct resource sets

### Wedge Success Metrics (Pre-Seed Pitch)

- "12 daily active users, 40% return within 24 hours"
- "Average 8 Places per user after 2 weeks"
- "Continue action used 3.2x/day per active user"
- "85% successful restoration rate (subjective survey)"

### Wedge → Platform Expansion

**Stage 1 (Current): Observe → Remember → Restore**  
Value: Time saved on context reconstruction

**Stage 2 (6-12 months): Understand → Anticipate**  
Value: "Here are resources related to this work"  
Value: "This task belongs to Project X"

**Stage 3 (12-24 months): Recognize Patterns → Suggest Templates**  
Value: "You do this workflow every Monday—automate it?"

**Stage 4 (24+ months): Execute → Automate**  
Value: Computer-use agent powered by witnessed behavioral data

**Key insight:** Each stage builds on the previous. Restoration requires understanding work. Understanding enables anticipation. Anticipation reveals patterns. Patterns enable automation. The wedge isn't separate from the platform—it's the foundation.

## 16. MAJOR RISKS

### Product Risks

**1. Restoration Reliability**  
**Risk:** If "Continue" fails 20% of the time, trust is broken and product is unusable  
**Mitigation:** Current MVP shows high success rate on supported apps; honest about limitations; graceful degradation  
**What pitch can address:** Demo of working restoration across multiple scenarios  
**What requires product proof:** 90%+ reliability at scale across diverse user workflows

**2. Cognitive Model Accuracy**  
**Risk:** If Places don't reflect actual work boundaries, product feels arbitrary  
**Mitigation:** Moment segmentation and Place detection improve with feedback loop  
**What pitch can address:** Explain the approach and learning mechanisms  
**What requires product proof:** User studies showing Place quality

### Technical Risks

**3. Platform Dependency**  
**Risk:** macOS Accessibility API changes break core functionality  
**Mitigation:** Platform-neutral architecture; multiple fallback mechanisms  
**What pitch can address:** Architecture separates platform-specific from cognitive core  
**What requires product proof:** Actually shipping Windows/Linux versions

**4. Performance & Resource Usage**  
**Risk:** Continuous observation drains battery or slows computer  
**Mitigation:** Lightweight collectors, efficient local processing  
**What pitch can address:** Technical approach to efficiency  
**What requires product proof:** Benchmark data showing minimal overhead

### Adoption Risks

**5. Privacy Perception**  
**Risk:** Users reject product due to observation/privacy concerns despite local-first architecture  
**Mitigation:** Local-first positioning, transparent about what's collected, encryption, never cloud by default  
**What pitch can address:** Proactive privacy story, contrast with Recall/Rewind  
**What requires product proof:** User testimonials, privacy audit, security review

**6. Behavior Change Requirements**  
**Risk:** Users don't trust automatic restoration and manually reconstruct anyway  
**Mitigation:** Restoration is optional (doesn't replace manual workflow), gradually builds trust  
**What pitch can address:** "Works alongside existing habits, doesn't require replacement"  
**What requires product proof:** Retention data showing sustained usage

### Competitive Risks

**7. Microsoft Copies This**  
**Risk:** If Evo proves the category, Microsoft integrates into Windows  
**Mitigation:** Speed of iteration, superior product quality, local-first vs cloud-first, cross-platform  
**What pitch can address:** "Recall's failure shows Microsoft can't ship this well; we can"  
**What requires product proof:** Undeniable product superiority and loyal user base

**8. Apple Builds This**  
**Risk:** Apple Continuity or Apple Intelligence adds work restoration  
**Mitigation:** Apple moves slowly; cross-platform is our advantage; acquisition potential  
**What pitch can address:** "We're building what Apple should have built; acquisition upside"  
**What requires product proof:** Mac-native quality that demonstrates we understand Apple users

### Distribution Risks

**9. Mac-Only Limits Growth**  
**Risk:** 30M Mac users isn't large enough for venture scale  
**Mitigation:** Windows and Linux expansion planned; Mac is wedge, not ceiling  
**What pitch can address:** Technical architecture supports cross-platform; Mac-first is deliberate strategy  
**What requires product proof:** Actually shipping cross-platform

**10. Discovery / Viral Growth**  
**Risk:** Product has no inherent viral loop; growth is slow and linear  
**Mitigation:** Product quality drives word-of-mouth; collaboration features create network effects  
**What pitch can address:** "Products this good in this category always find distribution (Notion, Superhuman, Raycast)"  
**What requires product proof:** Organic growth data, NPS scores, referral rates

### Monetization Risks

**11. Willingness to Pay**  
**Risk:** Users love free product but won't pay $10-20/mo  
**Mitigation:** Similar products successfully monetize this segment; value prop is clear time savings  
**What pitch can address:** Comps (Notion, Superhuman, Raycast) prove willingness to pay  
**What requires product proof:** Actual conversion data after paywall launch

### Existential Risk (Honest Assessment)

The highest risk is that **automatic work understanding is too hard to get right**.

If Places feel arbitrary, restoration feels unreliable, and the cognitive model doesn't match user mental models, Evo becomes "interesting tech without product-market fit."

The only answer is rapid iteration with real users and obsessive attention to product quality. Pre-seed is betting this is solvable—not that it's already solved.
