How it works

Three things happen, and then it is out of your way.

1

You work

Evo notices which window you are in, and which file it belongs to. Not pictures of the screen — just the window and the document.

2

You leave

The work becomes an object on the path, nearest first. Close the laptop. Nothing to save, nothing to name.

3

You come back

Say “Hey Evo” and name it, or press ⌘K. Evo opens the window, in the state you left it.

What it can do

Small, specific things. Everything else is deliberate silence.

Bring a window back

“Open the Q3 roadmap.” Evo finds the window and brings it forward, at the tab you were on.

Find what you half-remember

⌘K over everything you have open. Titles, sources, when you last touched it.

Say what happened

Activity shows what Evo did, when and why, with Undo. Nothing is invisible.

Stay quiet

Pause everything from the notch, the menu bar, or one shortcut. Capture stops at once.

Work offline

Voice and search run on this Mac. No account, no sync, no request leaving the machine.

Show its working

Diagnostics for the microphone, permissions, voice engine and storage — in plain words.

Questions

Short answers. The guide has the long ones.

Does Evo record my screen?

No. It reads the window you are in and the document it belongs to. There is no screen recording, no keystroke log.

Does anything leave my Mac?

No. Voice and text are handled on the device, and Evo has no account to sync to. Privacy and trust spells it out.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Evo is free to use.

Which macOS versions?

macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.

What if the voice engine fails?

The hotkey and typing still work. Evo says what happened and offers one action.
