# Cognitive Model

Status: Frozen
Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines the cognitive principles that make work continuity possible.

It does not describe how the human brain works.

It does not describe software.

It defines only the minimal assumptions required to understand why interrupted activity can later continue naturally.

Every architectural decision in Evo should be explainable from these principles.

---

# Scope

This model concerns continuity.

It does not attempt to explain perception, language, emotion, intelligence, consciousness, or general cognition.

Only the cognitive properties necessary for interruption and resumption are considered.

---

# Principle I — Human activity consists of engagements.

People continuously engage with the world.

An engagement is a coherent period of directed activity toward some part of reality.

Reading, writing, programming, conversation, research, planning, exploration, play and learning are all engagements.

The cognitive model makes no distinction between them.

---

# Principle II — Engagements are either open or closed.

Most engagements naturally conclude.

These are closed engagements.

Some engagements remain meaningfully continuable after attention leaves them.

These are open engagements.

Only open engagements create continuity across interruption.

Openness is a property of an engagement.

It is not a separate object.

---

# Principle III — Interruption does not necessarily end an engagement.

Attention is discontinuous.

Reality is continuous.

When attention leaves an open engagement, the engagement itself does not necessarily end.

It remains capable of meaningful continuation.

Time alone does not determine whether an engagement has ended.

---

# Principle IV — Continuity exists independently of recall.

People do not experience continuation because they perfectly remember the past.

They experience continuation because open engagements persist beyond interruption.

Memory supports continuity.

It does not create it.

---

# Principle V — Resumption restores capability, not memory.

Successful resumption is not exhaustive recall.

Successful resumption restores a person's ability to naturally produce the next meaningful action.

A person has resumed when continuing becomes easier than reconstructing.

The objective of resumption is capability.

Not recollection.

---

# Principle VI — External observers reason from evidence.

An observer never directly perceives another person's engagement.

An observer perceives only observable evidence produced through interaction with reality.

Understanding is therefore always inferential.

Different observers possess different evidence.

No observer possesses complete evidence.

---

# Principle VII — Inference is provisional.

Observable evidence is factual.

Interpretation is not.

Every explanation remains subject to revision as additional evidence becomes available.

Trustworthy systems preserve evidence while allowing interpretation to evolve.

---

# Principle VIII — Certain cognitive states are fundamentally private.

Intention.

Understanding.

Motivation.

Meaning.

Subjective importance.

These belong to the individual experiencing them.

They cannot be directly observed.

Any external explanation of them is necessarily inferential.

This is a fundamental epistemic boundary rather than an implementation limitation.

---

# Implications

The principles above imply the following.

Evidence should be preserved.

Interpretation should remain replaceable.

Observation and inference should never be confused.

Uncertainty should be represented honestly.

Assistance should reduce reconstruction effort without replacing human judgment.

Resumption should restore capability rather than reproduce history.

---

# Summary

Human activity consists of engagements.

Some engagements close naturally.

Others remain open across interruption.

Open engagements make continuity possible.

Observers cannot directly perceive engagement.

They infer it from observable evidence.

Resumption succeeds when a person regains the ability to naturally continue meaningful activity.

These principles form the cognitive foundation of Evo.
